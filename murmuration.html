Each starling follows only its nearest few neighbours, and the whole flock turns as one. Move your pointer through them to play the peregrine.

Seven neighbours

Field studies in Rome found each bird keeps track of a fixed number of neighbours, about six or seven, rather than everyone within a set distance. That is why a flock holds together while it stretches and thins.

Three rules

Steer away from birds that are too close. Match the heading of the ones nearby. Drift toward their centre. Put those three together, add a falcon, and the sky starts to boil.

Why at dusk

Flocks gather over the roost before settling for the night. Safety in numbers is the usual explanation. A peregrine struggles to pick one target out of a wheeling cloud of thousands.
